## How it works

Flatten converts each sample’s spatial and channel axes into one feature axis. It does not average, sort, or learn values. With channels-last ordering, the channel index varies fastest, then column, then row. Given the original shape, you can reconstruct the tensor. A mirrored image generally produces a different ordered vector.

## Worked example

A 2×2×1 tensor [[1,2],[3,4]] becomes [1,2,3,4]. Mirroring gives [2,1,4,3], not the same vector.

## Common misconception

Flatten has zero trainable parameters. Learned weights belong to subsequent layers.

## Equation

<Formula value={"k=(yW+x)C+c"} />

## Symbols

k: flat index; y,x: row and column; W: width; C: channels; c: channel index.

## Worked example

A 2×2×1 tensor [[1,2],[3,4]] becomes [1,2,3,4]. Mirroring gives [2,1,4,3], not the same vector.
