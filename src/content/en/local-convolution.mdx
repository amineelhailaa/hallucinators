## How it works

A convolution looks at local patches rather than connecting every pixel to every output. One kernel is reused across spatial locations. Each output is a neuron with a local weighted sum and bias. This sharing reduces parameters and lets a detector respond to the same local pattern in several locations. It does not make the entire classifier position-independent.

## Worked example

A 3×3 grayscale kernel has nine weights and one bias, whether it scans a 16×16 or a 64×64 image.

## Common misconception

The sliding kernel is learned during training; it does not gain a separate weight set at every position.
