## How it works

Dropout randomly zeros selected activations during training and rescales the retained ones. Standard inference disables this randomness. Batch normalization uses batch statistics in training and stored moving statistics in inference. Its scale and offset are trainable; moving statistics are state but not ordinary gradient-trained weights. Training mode therefore matters beyond weight updates.

## Worked example

With dropout rate 0.5, a retained activation 2 is scaled to 4 during training; the expected value remains 2.

## Common misconception

Calling a frozen model with training=True can still change batch-normalization behavior and activate dropout.

## Batch-normalization numerical example

For the two values [1,3], the batch mean is 2 and population variance is 1. Ignoring a tiny epsilon for this calculation, normalized values are [−1,1]. With γ=2 and β=1, outputs become [−1,3]. In inference, stored moving statistics replace these batch statistics; the same values may therefore give different outputs. Batch-normalization axes and statistics depend on the layer configuration.

<Formula value={"\\mu_B=\\frac1B\\sum_i x_i,\\quad \\sigma_B^2=\\frac1B\\sum_i(x_i-\\mu_B)^2"} />

B: number of values in the normalization group; μB: group mean; σB²: population variance.
</Section>
