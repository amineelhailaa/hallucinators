## How it works

Regression predicts numerical quantities. Our two linear outputs estimate normalized x and y positions. MSE emphasizes large residuals; MAE is less sensitive to outliers; Huber combines quadratic and linear regions. Output constraints should match the target domain. A linear head can predict outside [0,1], even when training targets are normalized.

## Worked example

Prediction [0.8,0.2], target [0.5,0.4] gives MSE=(0.09+0.04)/2=0.065 and MAE=0.25.

## Common misconception

A CNN is not restricted to classification. The output head and objective establish the task.

## Huber and the meaning of the objective

Let r be prediction minus target. Huber uses a quadratic region near zero and a linear region for larger residuals. At δ=1, r=0.5 gives 0.125 and r=2 gives 1.5. MSE is natural under a Gaussian error model; MAE relates to Laplace errors. These are modeling choices, not a prohibition on other losses. A classification model can optimize a squared error on appropriate targets, although cross-entropy generally provides a useful probabilistic objective.

<Formula value={"L_\\delta(r)=\\begin{cases}\\frac12r^2&|r|\\le\\delta\\\\\\delta(|r|-\\frac12\\delta)&|r|>\\delta\\end{cases}"} />

r: residual; δ: positive transition threshold; Lδ: Huber loss.
