## How it works

ReLU keeps positive inputs and clips negative ones to zero. Sigmoid maps to (0,1); tanh maps to (−1,1). ReLU is nonlinear globally even though each side is linear. Sigmoid and tanh saturate for large magnitudes. Choose activations for the role of the layer, not because one is universally best.

## Worked example

ReLU(−2)=0 and ReLU(3)=3. Its derivative is zero for negative inputs and one for positive inputs. At zero, implementations choose a convention.

## Common misconception

A ReLU neuron can remain inactive for all examples and receive zero gradient through its activation.
